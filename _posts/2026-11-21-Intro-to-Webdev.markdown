## Intro to Webdev  
  
Over the last couple of weeks I have taken a couple of tech deep dives. Across this journey, I have scratched some creative itches, and have also further cultivated what might be called my ‘cyber sovereignty’.   
  
Last week I spent nearly all of my spare time researching and assembling a relatively major upgrade to my home PC. (My graphics card went unchanged, but my cpu has over doubled in speed.) Truth be told, a large portion of this project was getting the aesthetics right, which itself included mostly cable management inside my Lian Li Dan A3 case; I wanted everything to look nice peering through the tempered glass side. I would say that function is still top priority, but even that rather superficial aspect of the hobby was highly rewarding in and of itself. I think it's important to curate a cosy space to create. 

I’m quite grateful that I was able to take the plunge and build a PC at this point in time, because, it is worth mentioning, we do live in an AI bubble which is impacting our world in wild and often unsavoury ways.  One hugely wide-reaching effect of this AI boom is that GPU prices (and now even SSD prices) have risen drastically to support the tremendous investment needed to keep this charade rolling. It’s all incredibly anti-consumer, as AI and crypto tycoons slurp up the world’s milkshake, so to say. As a result, the unfortunate reality is that the dream of building your own PC in 2026 has become more financially prohibitive than ever. Thankfully in my case, I was lucky enough to have already owned enough decent parts which were hackneyed into my 14 year old PC, meaning that I would only need to pump in about $980 AUD to get a really decent rig. (See footnotes for specs.)

Trying to be mindful about moving from one thing to the next, I’ve found that building this PC building has bleed into me now building a website (this website!). Yes, of course the irony is that I built the fancy sci-fi machine, and then the first thing I did with it could have been achieved with a potato and a ‘double-a’ battery. Nevertheless, I have been creating blogs for some years now (this is my third go I think), and much like me getting to finally build my own PC, I have been yearning for some time now to get my blog off of Wordpress and run it somehow free and open source. 

In the spirit of simplicity, this whole blog is hosted on Github, using Jekyll to edit locally and bring it to life. The trade off is that it is completely static compared to Wordpress - this means no comments, likes, and all of those usual ‘social network’ type features. It’s just text and hyperlinks. On the flipside, the benefit of me rolling this way is that I now really feel I own this blog. It’s much less reliant on for-profit companies, and other digital corporate ecosystems. There is no need for me to pay anything to keep it going, and no need for any future readers to pay either. And it feels much more lightweight, with a cleaner and simpler pipeline of making content and sharing it on the internet. 

Whilst no one will stumble upon this like they might if they were browsing Medium, I am very much approaching this with the knowledge that people will come; and not just ‘because I built it’, but rather because I know my standards and I know this will be quality. It’s a nice little confidence to know things are working for me. All in all, I will be more than happy to stay focussed on learning some light webdev, and, most importantly, the honing the way I communicate and put my thoughts into writing. 

Looking forward to writing more, and getting all jiggy and jazzy with it. 

Peace and love, 
-J
